How to read this table

The scores and thresholds below are Google's own published standards, not a ProPage opinion: Core Web Vitals and Lighthouse scoring.

Good Needs improvement Poor

  • Perf / A11y / Best pr. / SEO: Lighthouse's 0-100 grade in each area. 90-100 good, 50-89 needs improvement, 0-49 poor.
  • LCP (Largest Contentful Paint): how long the main content takes to appear on screen. Good under 2.5s, poor over 4s.
  • CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift): how much the page jumps around while it loads. Good under 0.1, poor over 0.25.
  • TBT (Total Blocking Time): how much the page freezes before you can click or scroll smoothly. Good under 200ms, poor over 600ms.

04

WCAG 2.1 AA accessibility

Lighthouse alone is not sufficient: it samples. Every text element on both pages was also loaded in real Chrome and its computed foreground checked against its composited background. Where text sits over a photo (the two hero sections), the DOM audit can only flag "needs manual review" since it can't read raster pixels — so those four elements were additionally measured pixel-for-pixel from real screenshots, described below.

DOM contrast audit

150 elements, 0 failures

Across both pages (141 home, 9 on 404 — up from 137 total, from the wider 4-column contact grid added this round). 7 elements sit over a photographic hero background and were flagged for manual review rather than guessed — resolved below.

  • Body text on cream: 6.8:1
  • Muted text on cream: 5.1:1
  • Accent buttons (ink on copper): 4.9:1
  • Nav links on header: 8.2:1

Hero text-over-photo, measured pixel-for-pixel

Each hero's text was hidden, the exact background pixels behind its bounding box screenshotted, and the single brightest pixel in that region (worst case, not an average) checked against the white text sitting on it.

  • Home H1 (45px bold, needs 3:1): 7.6:1 worst case
  • Home tagline (20px regular, needs 4.5:1): 6.1:1 worst case — darkened this round from a 4.67:1 near-miss margin (scrim 0.50 → 0.58 opacity)
  • New-market H1 (45px bold, needs 3:1): 5.9:1 worst case
  • New-market tagline (20px regular, needs 4.5:1): 5.9:1 worst case

Structure and interaction

  • Skip-to-main-content link, added this round (previously absent)
  • Semantic landmarks: header, nav, main, footer
  • :focus-visible outline added globally this round; previously only the contact form's inputs had any visible focus state — nav links, buttons and the theme toggle had none
  • Mobile menu sets aria-expanded on the toggle button
  • prefers-reduced-motion now honoured site-wide (added this round)
  • Theme toggle and nav toggle both carry aria-labels
  • Video lightbox closes on Escape and on backdrop click
  • All decorative SVG icons carry aria-hidden="true"
  • Contact form fields have visible placeholder labels and required marked

AI crawl and answer engine readiness

Two separate goals: stay legible to answer engines that cite sources, and opt out of being training data.

Readable by answer engines

  • Server-rendered HTML, no client-side rendering to wait for
  • Every fact sits in real text, not in an image
  • Named founder (Pooja Punjabi) and a named company, which is what a citation needs
  • Address, phone and email expressed in schema, not just prose

Training crawlers blocked

8 AI training user-agents disallowed in robots.txt (added this round; previously the file had no bot-specific rules at all), while search crawlers stay explicitly allowed:

GPTBotChatGPT-UserGoogle-ExtendedApplebot-ExtendedCCBotanthropic-aiClaudeBotPerplexityBotBytespider

Note that Google-Extended controls training use only. It does not affect normal Google Search indexing, which stays fully allowed.
