# Potbelly Audio — Photo Shot List for Pooja

Purpose: replace stock photography on the site with real photos of the studio and team, so the site
matches "human-led" and stops reading as generic/foreign. This is written to be forwarded to Pooja
as-is — she can shoot every one of these on a phone.

## How to shoot these (read once, applies to every card below)

**Camera / phone settings**
- Shoot **landscape** orientation (all cards are wide rectangles, not square or portrait).
- Turn the phone's grid lines ON (Settings → Camera → Grid). Use it to keep verticals straight.
- Tap the subject to focus, then hold and drag down slightly to underexpose a touch — the site's
  palette is warm and dark, so slightly moodier beats bright and flat.
- Turn HDR off if your phone forces a flat, over-lit look with it on — a single natural exposure
  reads more real.
- Never use flash. It flattens the image and is the fastest way to make a real photo look fake.
- Portrait Mode (blur) off unless a card specifically calls for it below — most of these need real
  depth from the room, not a software blur.
- Shoot each setup 5-6 times from slightly different angles/moments. Pick the best afterward, don't
  try to nail it in one frame.

**Light**
- Prefer whatever light is already in the room: window daylight, or the studio's own warm practical
  lamps. Avoid cold white/blue LED panel light and greenish tube-light — it clashes with the site's
  warm palette (terracotta `#c97a46`, warm cream, warm near-black) and reads "corporate stock photo."
- If you need to add light, use a warm desk lamp (2700-3000K, the "warm white" bulb setting, not
  "daylight" or "cool white") from the side or slightly above eye level, not straight-on.
- Console/gear screens glowing in an otherwise dim room look great — keep some of that if the studio
  already looks like that at night.

**People**
- Nobody should look at or smile at the camera. Shoot people mid-task, mid-conversation, mid-listen —
  candid, not posed. That alone is what separates "real" from "stock."
- Wardrobe: whatever people would normally wear to a session. No need to dress up.

**Sending files**
- Send full-resolution originals, not screenshots. On WhatsApp, attach as **Document**, not as
  Photo/Gallery — WhatsApp compresses regular photo sends and it shows on screen.
- If there are many, a shared Google Drive folder is easier than WhatsApp.
- No filters, no Instagram presets. Send it straight out of the camera; cropping and color matching
  to the site happens on our end.

## Section 1 — What We Do (9 cards)

### 1. Dubbing
- **Shot**: A voice artist mid-performance in the booth, looking at the screen/monitor (not the
  camera), mouth open or mid-line, headphones on.
- **Angle**: Slightly from the side, through the booth glass if possible (glass reflection is fine,
  even adds authenticity) or from just inside the door.
- **Light**: Studio's usual booth light. If it's a single warm bulb, that's ideal — keep it moody.
- **Frame**: Fill the frame with the artist and mic; don't leave a lot of empty booth wall.

### 2. Voiceovers
- **Shot**: Solo VO artist at the mic, 3/4 angle (not straight-on), captured mid-sentence.
- **Angle**: From behind/beside the mic stand looking past the pop filter at the artist — same
  framing style as most professional VO reels.
- **Light**: One warm key light or window light from the front-side; let the far side of the face
  fall into soft shadow for depth.
- **Frame**: Mic and pop filter in the foreground (slightly soft/blurred is fine), artist in focus
  behind it.

### 3. Translation & Localisation
- **Shot**: A translator's desk, mid-work — script pages with visible red-pen markup or highlighted
  lines, laptop open beside them showing the working document, hand holding a pen over the page.
- **Angle**: Top-down or steep 3/4 angle over the desk, phone held above looking down.
- **Light**: Natural desk/window light, no overhead fluorescent glare on the paper.
- **Frame**: Hands + script + laptop screen edge all in frame; keep the laptop content generic/
  non-confidential.

### 4. Casting
- **Shot**: Either (a) a casting sheet — printed voice-talent shortlist or headshots — spread on a
  desk next to an open laptop, with a hand pointing at one entry, or (b) a talent auditioning at the
  mic with someone listening in the background, slightly out of focus.
- **Angle**: (a) top-down over the desk. (b) shoot past the listener's shoulder toward the talent at
  the mic.
- **Light**: Same as Translation for (a); same as Voiceovers for (b).

### 5. Dubbing Direction
- **Shot**: The classic through-the-glass shot — a director in the control room gesturing or speaking
  into the talkback mic, with the booth and artist visible (soft-focus) through the glass behind them.
- **Angle**: From inside the control room, slightly behind/beside the director, glass and booth in
  the background.
- **Light**: Console/monitor glow lighting the director's face from the front is ideal — very
  "real studio at night" feeling.
- **Frame**: Director in sharp focus in the foreground third, booth/artist soft in the background.

### 6. Recording & Mixing
- **Shot**: Close-up of hands on a mixing console fader or a DAW (Pro Tools/Reaper/whatever's used)
  timeline on screen, mid-adjustment.
- **Angle**: Low, close, from the side — hands and console/screen fill most of the frame.
- **Light**: Screen glow + one warm accent light; let the rest of the room go dark.
- **Frame**: Tight crop. This should feel like a detail shot, not a wide room shot.

### 7. Audio Post-Production
- **Shot**: Over-the-shoulder of an editor at the screen, waveform or timeline visible, editor's hand
  on mouse/trackpad.
- **Angle**: Directly behind the editor's shoulder, screen readable but not the main subject — the
  person working is.
- **Light**: Same screen-glow-plus-warm-accent approach as Recording & Mixing.

### 8. Quality Control
- **Shot**: Someone with headphones on, eyes closed or focused downward, clearly listening
  critically — not performing, reviewing. A QC checklist or script with tick marks nearby is a nice
  optional detail.
- **Angle**: Close 3/4 face shot, shallow depth if the phone's portrait mode looks natural (this is
  one card where a soft background blur is fine).
- **Light**: Soft, warm, front-side light — this should feel calm and focused, not dramatic.

### 9. Built for Volume
- **Shot**: A wide shot that shows scale — multiple booths/stations in use at once, or a corridor
  with more than one studio door, or the team working in parallel. This is the one card that should
  feel wide and busy, in contrast to the tight/intimate framing of the other eight.
- **Angle**: Wide, from a doorway or corridor, capturing depth (several points of activity front to
  back).
- **Light**: Whatever's naturally on in the space at that time — don't over-light this one, the
  business/bustle should read as ordinary and real.

## Section 2 — Why Potbelly? (6 cards)

### 10. Experience
- **Shot**: A senior engineer or Pooja at a console/desk they clearly know well — hands confidently
  on the gear, mid-adjustment, not posed for a portrait.
- **Angle**: 3/4 angle at the console, similar feel to Recording & Mixing but can include more of the
  person (waist-up) since this card is about the person's experience, not just the gear.
- **Light**: Warm, console-glow style, matching cards 5-7.

### 11. The Right People
- **Shot**: A genuine group shot — two or three team members together (e.g. a translator, an
  engineer, a director) in conversation or working side by side. Not a lineup, not everyone facing
  camera.
- **Angle**: Candid, slightly wide, capture them mid-interaction.
- **Light**: Natural room light is fine here — this card is about people, not mood.

### 12. Scale
- **Shot**: An establishing shot that reads "more than one studio" — three studio doors down a
  corridor, or a wide shot of one full studio (booth + control room together) showing it's a real,
  substantial space.
- **Angle**: Wide, from a fixed point that shows depth.
- **Light**: Natural.

### 13. One Point of Contact
- **Shot**: Someone (could be Pooja, could be a project coordinator) on a call or video call at a
  laptop, headset on, clearly coordinating — the "one person managing everything" feeling.
- **Angle**: 3/4 angle at the desk, laptop and headset visible.
- **Light**: Warm desk-lamp light, same family as the Translation card.

### 14. We Get the Original
- **Shot**: Hands referencing an original script or video still next to the localised draft — showing
  the comparison/checking-against-source process. A monitor showing a paused video frame next to a
  printed or on-screen script works well.
- **Angle**: Top-down or steep 3/4 over the desk, similar to Translation.
- **Light**: Natural desk light.

### 15. Quality Matters
- **Shot**: Different from Quality Control (card 8) — this one should show the checking artifact
  itself: a marked-up QC sheet or script with visible tick marks/corrections, headphones resting
  beside it (not worn), pen in hand mid-mark.
- **Angle**: Top-down close-up on the sheet and pen, headphones at the edge of frame.
- **Light**: Natural desk light.
