### A3. What the whole category does, and where the gap is

Consistent across every competitor examined:

1. **They compete on the language count.** 60+, 100+, 200+, 250+. It is the headline number on
   almost every site, and it is an arms race Potbelly cannot win and should not enter.
2. **They compete on studio count and certification.** Facility as proof.
3. **Almost none of them let you hear anything.** Dubbing and voice-over sites, selling work that
   exists only as sound, and the buyer is asked to read about it. Showreels are usually absent,
   buried, or a YouTube link that leaves the site.
4. **The work is anonymous.** Logos and category claims, rarely a named piece of work you can play.
5. **Nobody is a person.** Corporate voice throughout, no named director, no founder, no ear you can
   trust by name.

**The differentiation angle.** Potbelly's advantage is not scale, and pretending otherwise puts her
in a fight she loses. It is that the work can be heard, and that a working dubbing artist runs the
place and stays on the project. The category sells capacity in the abstract. She can sell a
performance you can play in the browser, chosen and directed by a named person who answers the
WhatsApp message herself. Her brief already says this: "Our work speaks for itself", "One point of
contact", "We get the original". The site's job is to make the first of those literally true, which
is why the work section is the centre of the page and not a portfolio afterthought.

**A note on the language count.** Her eleven named languages are fewer than the numbers competitors
advertise. Do not inflate and do not apologise. Named languages with native script beat an
unsubstantiated 200+, and "Don't see your language? Ask Us" handles the rest exactly as she wrote it.

## B. Buyer evaluation criteria

From live search on how OTT and content buyers select dubbing and localisation vendors in 2026.
Each is labelled for what we do with it.

| What buyers check | Use |
|---|---|
| Crew roster and current capacity | **PROOF POINT.** Her "Built for Volume" and "The Right People" already answer it. Needs her numbers to be specific |
| Evidence of comparable technical demands, for example Atmos delivery for streaming | **ASK THE OWNER.** We do not know her delivery specs. Currently published as fact on the live site with no source |
| Lip-sync accuracy and how it is checked | **FAQ ENTRY.** Her "multiple rounds of QC, at script level and again after recording" is the honest answer |
| Culturally appropriate pacing, tone and emotional delivery, not literal translation | **PROOF POINT.** This is precisely her "We get the original" and "not just words". The single strongest alignment between what buyers say they want and what she already wrote |
| Security and NDA handling before delivery | **ASK THE OWNER.** Enterprise buyers ask this early. Currently unanswered anywhere |
| Turnaround, in days | **ASK THE OWNER.** The live site publishes 7-14, 3-7 and 1-2 day tiers with no source. Withheld until she confirms |
| Language coverage | **PROOF POINT.** The language chips |
| Position on AI dubbing | **ASK THE OWNER, then FAQ ENTRY.** Platform acceptance varies by streamer and premium originals often still require human performance. Buyers ask directly in 2026. Saying nothing reads as hiding. Her answer, whichever way it goes, is a differentiator |
| Pricing model | **FAQ ENTRY, no numbers.** Published benchmarks exist for the category but they are not her rates and must never be presented as hers |
| Streamer-approved credits, vendor track record | **PROOF POINT once permissions are confirmed.** The seven videos on her own channel are the start |

## E. Objections, ranked, which become the FAQ

1. Can a boutique handle our volume? (Her answer: Built for Volume. Needs her studio and team numbers)
2. How fast? (ASK HER. No number ships until she gives one)
3. Which languages, really? (Answered by the chips, honestly, without inflation)
4. Will it sound native or will it sound dubbed? (Her strongest answer: "We get the original")
5. How is quality actually checked? (Multiple QC rounds, script level and post-recording. Sourced)
6. Who picks the voices, and can we? (ASK HER)
7. What does it cost, and how is it priced? (Model only, never a number)
8. Do you use AI? (ASK HER. Must be answered one way or the other)
9. How is our content kept secure before release? (ASK HER)
10. What formats do you deliver? (ASK HER. Currently published unsourced)
11. Who do we deal with day to day? (One point of contact. Sourced)
12. How does a project start? (Process, written from her workflow, not invented)

Items marked ASK HER are in `docs/client-questions-2026-08-18.md`. Six of the twelve most common
buyer questions currently have answers published on the live site that nobody can source. That is the
single largest content risk in this rebuild, and it is why the claims register gates the copy.
