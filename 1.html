How a project runs

One brief in. One delivery out. No handoffs in between.

Localisation is three different jobs wearing one name: a language job, a performance job and a sound job. Split it across three vendors and the seams show up in the finished file. All three happen here.

The script stage

A weak script cannot be rescued by a strong actor.

Everything upstream of the booth happens here. Translation, adaptation and quality control on the page, while a fix still costs one line instead of a whole session.

00:01

Translation & localisation

Translation that reads for context, culture, tone and audience, not just words. The test is whether a native speaker forgets they are watching a dub.

00:02

Script-level QC

Every script clears quality control before recording starts. A problem caught on the page costs a line. The same problem caught after the mix costs the session.

00:03

Terms & continuity

Names, terminology and running references kept consistent across an episode, a season and a whole catalogue, so nothing drifts between batches.

The voice stage

Casting is a decision you can hear.

Localisation should make content feel native, not make it feel translated. That is settled in casting and in direction long before it becomes a technical question, which is why a director is in the room for the session.

00:04

Casting

A wide pool of voice talent across languages, age groups and performance styles, with options sent back so you can hear the choice instead of reading it.

00:05

Dubbing direction

Experienced dubbing directors who know how to get the right performance out of an artist, take after take, across a full season.

00:06

Dubbing

Natural, well directed dubbing that keeps the performance, emotion and intent of the original, in sync and in character.

00:07

Voiceovers

The right voice for films, documentaries, advertising, corporate and digital content, briefed properly and delivered ready to cut.
